# INT-01: Teacher Intervention Draft

**DEMO MODE - synthetic data**
**Status:** DRAFT. The teacher reviews and publishes the final version through Person 2's interface. Person 1 enforces approval in the live service.
**Length:** about 60-90 seconds spoken.
**Concept:** v = 0 versus a = g downward at the top of a throw.

## Spoken explanation

"Many of us said the acceleration at the top is zero, because the ball stops for a moment. Let's separate two ideas: velocity and acceleration.

Velocity tells us how fast and which way the ball moves right now. At the very top, that is zero, just for an instant.

Acceleration tells us how the velocity is *changing*. On the way up, the upward velocity gets smaller. On the way down, the downward velocity gets bigger. The velocity changes by about 9.8 meters per second, every second, in the downward direction. That change does not pause at the top. Gravity is still acting, so the acceleration is still about 9.8 meters per second squared, downward.

If the acceleration really were zero at the top, the ball would stay there, hovering. But it doesn't. It starts falling right away."

## One simple visual

Draw a vertical line with five snapshots of the ball, bottom to top to bottom. Beside each, draw a **velocity arrow**: long up, short up, *no arrow* at the top, short down, long down. Next to every snapshot draw the **same small downward arrow labeled "g"**. Point out that the velocity arrow changes while the g arrow never does.

## One Socratic prompt

> "If the acceleration were zero at the top, what would the ball do next?"
